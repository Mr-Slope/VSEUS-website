The Economics Learning Centre is the most underused thing the society runs. It's free,
you don't need an appointment, and the person helping you took the same course, often
with the same professor.

## The basics

- **Where:** IONA Room 038
- **When:** Monday to Thursday, 11:00am to 5:00pm
- **What to bring:** your UBC Student ID
- **Cost:** nothing

Walk in, check in at the front, and work with a tutor on a first-come, first-served
basis. Check-in takes under a minute.

## Courses we cover

Our tutors are trained across ECON 101, 102, 226, 301, 302, 325, and 326: the
first-year principles courses, econometrics, and intermediate micro and macro.

Have a question from a course that isn't on that list? Bring it anyway. Tutors will do
their best, and if they can't help they'll tell you straight away rather than waste your
afternoon.

## How to get more out of a session

Turning up is most of it. But a few things make the difference between a useful hour and
a frustrating one:

1. **Bring a specific question.** "I don't understand chapter 4" is hard to help with.
   "I can set up this Lagrangian but I don't know why we take this derivative" is easy.
2. **Bring your attempt, not a blank page.** Even a wrong attempt tells the tutor where
   your understanding breaks down. That's the whole diagnosis.
3. **Come before the week of the midterm.** The centre is quiet in week 3 and packed in
   week 6. The same hour of help is worth more when you have time to use it.
4. **Ask why, not just how.** You can get the answer from a solutions manual. What you
   can't get from a manual is someone explaining the reasoning until it clicks.

> A tutor's job is not to do the problem set. It's to make the next one easier.
